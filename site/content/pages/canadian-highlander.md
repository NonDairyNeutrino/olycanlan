## What is Canadian Highlander?

Canadian Highlander (often shortened to "CanLander") is a singleton, 1v1 Magic: The Gathering format built for high-powered, efficient games. It shares Commander's "one copy of each card" philosophy but drops the Commander itself and color-identity restrictions, running instead on the Vintage ban list with its own points system layered on top.

### The Core Rules

- **Singleton** — Only one copy of any card allowed, except basic lands and cards that explicitly permit multiples (e.g. *Relentless Rats*).
- **100-card minimum deck**, no maximum, no Commander. (You can run more than 100, but most players don't — bigger decks just mean less consistency for no extra points.)
- **20 starting life**, standard 1v1 rules otherwise.
- **Vintage ban list** — nothing is banned for being "too powerful." Cards like Black Lotus, the Power Nine, and Time Walk are all legal.
- **The Points List** — instead of banning powerful cards outright, the format assigns them a point value (typically 1–8 points), and each deck gets **10 points to spend per 100 cards**. Want to run Black Lotus (7 points)? That's most of your budget gone. Want a fistful of 1-point staples instead? Also an option.

The Points List is maintained by the Canadian Highlander Council and updated a few times a year as the format's metagame shifts. Since our league follows the official list, always check the current version before finalizing a decklist for submission.

### Why Play It?

Canadian Highlander tends to reward tight, efficient deckbuilding — you're free to run the most powerful cards in Magic's history, but the points system forces real trade-offs about which ones make the cut. Games are fast, decisions matter, and there's a huge design space since almost nothing is flat-out banned.
